# LinkedIn Growth Playbook for B2B SaaS

Quick note before you read this,
I looked at all 10 experts I researched. For them I read thier LinkedIn posts myself. For 2 of them (Adam Robinson and Lara Acosta) I watched full YouTube videos and pulled from those instead, since they don't post much text content.
Not everything I found was actually useful for a LinkedIn growth playbook, even though it was real content. Santosh Sarangi mostly talks about Google Ads, not LinkedIn. Amelia Sordell's best post was about hiring, not LinkedIn growth. Shweta Pandey's posts were mostly event recaps and a company announcement. Nancy Pezarkar's posts were generic motivational stuff. Shreya Jain had one post about account targeting (not really about LinkedIn content) and one post about using AI properly (which is useful). I'm being honest about this instead of pretending every expert gave me something equally solid - some did, some didn't and I've said which is which below.

## Recommendations (with sources)

Don't try to perfect your posting schedule or format before you've been posting regularly for a while. If you only post once in a while, you don't get enough data to know what's actually working for your audience.(source: Rosanna Campbell, LinkedIn post, 07.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/3phwjx5w)

Write posts that say “here's what I did” instead of “here's how to do it.” Real stories beat generic advice. Also, put the actual value inside the post — don't make people click somewhere else to get it. (source: Rosanna Campbell, LinkedIn post, 07.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/3phwjx5w)

Good B2B posts usually make the reader feel one of four things: smarter, safer in their job, able to win an argument with their boss, or like they don't have to do some boring task anymore. Think about what the reader wants before you think about what you want to say. (source: Rosanna Campbell, LinkedIn post, 05.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/yutey8xu)

Try a 60/30/10 mix: 60% posts about you (who you are, what you've been through), 30% posts showing your skills/expertise, 10% posts that push people to actually buy or take action. One of his clients switched to posting only expertise content and their likes dropped from 63 to 17 per post. (source: Matt Barker, LinkedIn post, 08.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/2a5ncfkw)

Your first posts will probably flop. His first three posts got 22, 6, and 2 likes. It wasn't until his 97th post that things really took off (196 likes). Don't quit early. (source: Matt Barker, LinkedIn post, 05.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/3bhaseu3)

If you use AI to help write posts, always rewrite it in your own words — use it to speed yourself up, not replace yourself. People can spot the lazy AI patterns (like “No X. No Y. Just Z.”) and it makes you look low-effort. (source: Matt Barker, LinkedIn post, 03.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/4pubres8)

Before building any kind of content strategy, talk to at least 50 potential customers first to check people will actually pay for what you're offering — not just say they like the idea. (source: Adam Robinson, YouTube, “How I’d Start a SaaS From Zero,” https://youtube.com/watch?v=r47Q4Cfit5s)

Pick one small, specific topic and stick to it instead of talking about a whole broad industry. If you talk about “marketing” in general, you're competing with huge accounts. Go narrow instead. (source: Lara Acosta, YouTube, “How I Grew to 100K Followers,” https://youtube.com/watch?v=ZK-CBIFDR9w)

When you ask AI to write something, give it real context first — who you're writing for, what you're trying to say, what tone to use, what to avoid. Don't just say “write a LinkedIn post about X” and expect something good back. AI can't guess your audience or your voice on its own. (source: Shreya Jain, LinkedIn post, 04.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/3zsn3w8t)

As of 2026, LinkedIn is still the strongest platform for getting B2B leads — about 80% of B2B social leads reportedly come from it, with a 121% return on ad spend reported in 2025. Pairing LinkedIn with TikTok or X for awareness reportedly did even better (2.5x the return of using LinkedIn alone). (source: originally from Draper, shared in Shreya Jain's LinkedIn roundup post, 06.07.2026, https://tinyurl.com/3phwjx5w — I haven't independently checked Draper's original numbers, just passing along what was cited)

## Where the experts disagree

1. Should most of your content be about you, or about your expertise?
Matt Barker says: post mostly about yourself (60%) and less about your specific skills (30%). His most-viewed posts in 2026 were personal ones — about becoming a dad, about Christmas, about his revenue. And when a client of his switched to posting only expertise content, their engagement got cut by more than half (63 likes down to 17).

Lara Acosta says: pick one very specific topic and stay there, instead of talking broadly. Her reasoning is that broad topics mean you're competing with way bigger accounts.
Which one I'd follow, and why: I lean more toward Matt Barker here, mostly because he has an actual before/after result to back it up, not just an opinion. I don't think these two ideas are fully opposite, though — I think Lara's advice is really about what your niche 30% should be about, not an argument to drop personal content completely. But if I had to pick where they truly conflict (how much of your content should be personal vs. topic-focused), I'd lean toward more personal content than a pure-niche approach.

## 2. Does posting more often actually help?

Rosanna Campbell says: post a lot, even if it's not perfect, because posting rarely means you never get enough data to figure out what works for your specific audience.
Sameer Alam says: posting more often is a myth — he says quality matters more than frequency, and posting a lot of low-value stuff won't get you anywhere.

Which one I'd follow, and why: Rosanna's, mostly. I don't think they fully disagree — Rosanna is talking about a learning phase early on, and Sameer is talking about long-term quality. But where they do clash, I trust Rosanna more. She backs it up with 10 years of hands-on experience, while Sameer just states his opinion as fact with nothing behind it. And as you'll see below, some of Sameer's other posts have numbers that don't check out, which makes me trust his other claims less too.

## 3. Should you lean into AI for content, or be careful with it?

Matt Barker says: only use AI to speed up your own writing, and always edit it into your own words. He specifically calls out lazy, obviously-AI-written posts as a sign you're hiding your own voice.

Sameer Alam says: go all-in on AI-driven marketing — he claims a 37% jump in ROI and says AI marketing is “4x more effective” than regular marketing.

Which one I'd follow, and why: Matt Barker's, easily. The funny part is that Sameer's own post about AI marketing is written in exactly the repetitive, robotic style Matt warns about (he repeats “the real secret to unlocking ai driven growth is X” three times in a row), and none of his stats have a source behind them. So the post kind of proves Matt's point by accident. Shreya Jain backs up Matt's side too, in a separate post about how to actually get good results from AI — she wasn't responding to either of them, but she's saying basically the same thing.

## 4. Should you reach out to people at scale, or keep it personal?

Adam Robinson says: use semi-automated outreach — sending 10 to 50 connection requests a day, using tools like Hey Reach or Growth Machine, plus cold email at scale.
Lara Acosta says: skip the high-volume outreach entirely. Build relationships slowly instead, through commenting on posts and doing 1:1 coffee chats.

Which one I'd follow, and why: Lara's approach, especially for someone doing this without a big team or budget behind them. Automated outreach on LinkedIn can actually get your account flagged or restricted — Adam even says so himself in his own video — so it's a real risk for one person trying to build a personal brand.

## What I decided not to include, and why

1. Sameer Alam's AI marketing stats
His numbers (37% ROI increase, “4x more effective”) don't have any source or explanation behind them anywhere in the post. And the post itself is written in the same repetitive, robotic style that other sources warn is a sign of low-effort AI content. So I didn't feel comfortable including these numbers as real advice.

2. Adam Robinson's cold email setup (buying lots of domains)
He recommends buying and rotating hundreds of domains to keep your cold email deliverability high. That's built for a funded company doing large-scale outreach, not for one person building a LinkedIn presence. It's too much infrastructure for what this playbook is actually for.

3. Santosh Sarangi's Google Ads strategy
Honestly, this was genuinely good, well-explained advice — a 3-stage plan for setting your ad budget based on how many conversions you're getting. But it's about Google Ads, not LinkedIn, so it doesn't belong in a LinkedIn playbook even though the source is solid.


## My own idea (not from any source)

A simple checklist before you hit publish
Nobody I researched said this exact thing, but I think it's a useful mashup of two ideas above. Before posting anything, run it through two quick checks: First, which of Matt Barker's three buckets does this post fall into (about you, about your skills, or a direct pitch) — and are you roughly keeping the 60/30/10 mix over the month? Second, does the post actually deliver one of Rosanna Campbell's four things (make the reader smarter, help their job, help them win an argument, or save them time)? If a post doesn't clearly do either of those things, don't post it yet — go back and fix it. I think this could work because it turns two vague “write good content” ideas into something you can actually check off, which is more useful for someone who doesn't have a natural feel for what makes a post good yet.

## Where this playbook falls short

●	Even with real posts in hand, not every expert actually gave me something about LinkedIn growth. Santosh Sarangi is about Google Ads, Amelia Sordell's best post is about hiring, Shweta Pandey's posts are mostly event recaps and a company announcement, and Shreya Jain's account-targeting post isn't about content at all. So really only 6-7 of the 10 experts gave me stuff I could actually use here.

●	Sameer Alam's numbers don't check out, which makes me trust his other posts less too, even the ones that sound reasonable.

●	The LinkedIn stat I used from Shreya Jain's post is two steps removed from the original source — it's Draper's number, shared by Shreya, shared by me. I haven't checked Draper's actual research myself.

●	All the posts I looked at are from a pretty short window, late June to early August 2026. I have no idea if any of this still works six months or a year from now, or after the next LinkedIn algorithm change.

●	Both of the YouTube experts (Adam Robinson, Lara Acosta) are telling their own success story after the fact, and their follower/revenue numbers aren't checked by anyone outside their own claims.

●	My two strongest sources are both Western creators. Santosh Sarangi could've filled the India/B2B SaaS gap, but his real content ended up being about ads, not LinkedIn content, so that gap is still mostly open.

## Who I wouldn't recommend following, and why

**Sameer Alam**
A few of his posts have very specific numbers (37% ROI increase, “4x more effective,” 99.2% accuracy on some tool) with zero explanation of where they came from. His writing style also reads like it was generated by AI without much editing. And one post is really just an ad for his own tool (InfluencerHub) dressed up as advice. All of that together made me not trust his content.

**Adam Robinson** — but only partly
His thinking on product-market fit and how to sequence your content is genuinely good. But I wouldn't follow his outreach-automation advice — he even mentions in his own video that people doing this at scale have gotten warnings on their accounts. If the person giving the advice says it's risky, I'm not going to recommend it as safe.

**Nancy Pezarkar**
Nothing wrong or untrustworthy here — her posts just aren't useful for learning LinkedIn strategy specifically. They're general motivational posts about staying true to your goals and building trust, the kind of thing that could apply to literally any platform or job. If you want actual LinkedIn tactics, there's nothing here to use.
